What Is Really Causing Your Breakouts

Acne forms when excess oil (sebum), dead skin cells clogging the pore, C. acnes bacteria, and inflammation converge. The specific trigger mix varies from person to person:

Hormonal fluctuations commonly drive adult breakouts along the jawline, chin, and lower cheeks.

Excess oil production (often genetic or stress-linked) feeds clogged pores and deep, tender cysts prone to scarring.

Cellular buildup occurs when surface cells do not shed efficiently, trapping oil and debris inside the follicle.

Inflammation turns a quiet clog into a painful red blemish and leads to lingering post-breakout marks.

Why Over-the-Counter Products Often Fall Short

Salicylic acid and benzoyl peroxide from the drugstore can help mild breakouts. For moderate, persistent, hormonal, or cystic acne, however, over-the-counter routines tend to plateau, or worse, harsh scrubs and drying spot treatments strip your skin barrier and worsen inflammation.

Professional care shifts the strategy from chasing individual blemishes to calming active breakouts, regulating pore turnover, and preventing long-term scarring.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long until I see clearer skin?

Many patients notice early improvement within 4 to 6 weeks, while deeper or hormonal acne typically takes several months of consistent care to settle completely.

Is adult acne treated differently from teenage acne?

Often yes. Adult acne frequently involves hormonal shifts and barrier sensitivity, so plans focus on regulating inflammation and turnover gently rather than stripping oil aggressively.

Will treatment help with acne scarring too?

Yes. Once active breakouts calm down, chemical peels and microneedling or RF microneedling significantly improve lingering marks and textural pitting.

Can I wear makeup during treatment?

Generally yes, using non-comedogenic formulas. Your provider will let you know about any brief pauses right after an in-office peel or microneedling session.
